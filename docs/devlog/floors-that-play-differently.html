Floors that play differently

The owner said every floor played like the last. One day's answer: floor jobs and memos, a blocked door found and fixed, combo and ultimate without buttons to look for, techniques in place of the cards, and a faster way back from a wrong turn.

Day 8
DesignFloorsControlsPlaytest

The owner played the TestFlight build and came back with a list. Floors feel the same. A door on floor 6 would not let Rin through. The combo and the ultimate make you look away from the fight. The cards are confusing. A wrong turn costs a long walk back. This post is that list, taken in order.

Why the floors felt the same

The cause was simple once it was looked for. An ordinary floor only ever asked one thing: reach the stairs. Nothing had to be fought and nothing had to be found. Of the 79 floors without a guardian or a story scene, about 55 to 60 differed only by their random layout.

So each block of five floors now runs in a rhythm: a plain floor, a floor with a job, a floor under a memo, a job under a memo, then the guardian.

Jobs keep the stairs shut until they are done. Each part of the tower gives them its own name, and the building's PA reads them out in its usual tone.

  • Collect: three things in three rooms far apart. Timesheets on the Desk, bedside charts in the Clinic, coat tickets at the Gala.
  • Keyholder: one tough resident carries the stair keycard. The Desk calls it a badge audit.
  • Headcount: cut down half the floor.
  • Hold: thirty seconds on the stair landing while more of them come through its doors.
  • Drill: reach the stairs inside a time for a full ultimate and some health. Being late is only noted on your file.

Memos change the whole floor: Overtime (more of them, more drops), Casual Friday (faster and weaker), Power Saving (the lights at a third), Bonus Season, Wet Floor, and Restructuring (half as many, each twice as tough).

The 31 new PA lines were voiced the same day, at the owner's word.

A door that would not open

The owner could not get into the last room on floor 6. The guess was that the stairs had been built across the entrance, and the guess was right. The flight of stairs is solid, six metres long. In a small room, or one with doors on both far walls, it was placed in the middle of the room, running into the corner, and nothing checked that against the doors.

A test over 200 towers put a number on it: the stairs stood in a doorway on 1,638 of 30,000 floors, about one in eighteen. The stairs now have to leave every door clear, and the same test finds none blocked.

Eyes on the fight

The combo and the ultimate each had a small button that appeared near the right thumb. To use one you had to find it, and to find it you had to stop watching the enemies. The owner often missed the combo because of that.

The owner suggested two thumbs for the ultimate and pointed at two games to study: the Batman Arkham series and Ghost of Tsushima. Both put the cue on the enemy, never in a corner of the screen, and Ghost of Tsushima starts its black-and-white killing run by pressing both sticks at once.

  • Ultimate: sweep both thumbs up together, anywhere on the screen. When it is ready, arrows pulse under each thumb.
  • Combo: there is no button. A ring closes on an enemy; tap anywhere when it turns gold. Too early loses the chain. The phone buzzes as the ring turns.
  • Long chains: from the third leap in a row, the room drains to black and white until the chain ends.

Techniques, not cards

Between floors the game dealt poker cards, and the hand you held changed how Rin fought. The owner found it confusing, and felt it did not fit a game sold on one sword and a hundred floors. There should be no new gear. The sword itself should grow.

So the cards are switched off, and each guardian now teaches the sword one technique. The first six were already in the game, hidden behind which hand you held. Every one pays only when you dodge or counter well, so none is a flat number to balance around.

| Guardian's floor | Technique | What it does | |---|---|---| | 5 | Riposte | Roll through a blow and the next cut hits much harder | | 10 | Counter-cut | Double damage on an enemy winding up | | 15 | Borrowed Breath | A timed roll gives stamina back | | 20 | Long Draw | After such a roll, the next heavy cut reaches further | | 25 | Last Stand | Survive one killing blow a floor | | 30 | Dawn Cut | Once a floor, a full string of cuts fills the ultimate |

The stair between floors is now one page: what the guardian taught, and a button to climb.

The way back

The owner asked whether Rin could cut through walls to save the walk back from a dead end. That would also cut past locked stairs and every job that sends her to find something. Two smaller changes went in instead. When nothing nearby wants a fight, Rin runs about half as fast again. And the rooms furthest off the way to the stairs now hold something worth the walk.

What has not been checked

Every clip on this page was played by the game's bot on a Mac. That shows the pieces work. It does not show that they are fun.

  • Nobody has played any of this on a phone. The two-thumb ultimate and the tap-on-gold combo are touch gestures, and the bot does not use touch at all.
  • Balance is untested. Without the cards' bonuses the early floors are a little harder, and nobody knows yet whether thirty seconds on the landing is fair.
  • Rin has no running animation. Her walk is played faster for the sprint, and it may look wrong.
  • Only the first six guardians teach a technique. New moves of the sword's own, such as a cut straight out of a roll, are the next stage.
  • None of it is in the beta yet. The builds on TestFlight and itch.io are still the older ones.

The Android build and the game's page are on itch.io; the iPhone beta is on TestFlight.
